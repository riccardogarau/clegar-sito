Due diligence on an inherited dataset: a cable’s burial cover has a date

In a technical due diligence on an operating wind farm, the data room contains the export cable as-built report: minimum cover 1.10 m, above the design minimum of 1.0 m everywhere. The statement is true. But it describes 2021, and the acquisition is signed in 2026.

A dataset that comes with the asset does not describe the asset: it describes the asset on the date it was acquired. For many quantities the difference does not matter, because they do not change in five years. The cover over a buried cable is different: it is the difference between two surfaces, and one of the two moves.

A difference between two surfaces

Cover is the vertical distance between the seabed and the top of the cable. Once buried, the cable stays where it is. The seabed does not: in a sand wave field crests and troughs migrate, and where a trough arrives over the cable the cover falls without the cable having moved.

Hence one opportunity and one constraint. The opportunity: if the absolute level of the top of the cable is known, a new bathymetric survey is enough to update the cover, without finding the cable again. The constraint: it has to be the absolute level, referred to a stated vertical datum. A report that gives only the cover – one number per section, measured against the seabed of the day – cannot be updated by any later survey. To know how much cover remains, the cable then has to be found again, with a tracking system and a campaign much longer than a bathymetric survey.

A worked example

The figures below are illustrative – built to show the mechanism, not taken from real projects – but the proportions are those of a real site.

The export cable crosses a sand wave field between KP 12 and KP 20. The design criterion is a minimum cover of 1.0 m relative to the seabed at the time of installation. The data room contains the 2021 as-built report and also the file of cable top levels, referred to LAT. The buyer has a multibeam survey of the corridor carried out.

Before subtracting the two surfaces, one check is needed. Two surveys from different years and contractors can differ by a vertical constant – tidal reduction, reference, draft – that has nothing to do with the seabed. They are therefore compared over a section that does not move: between KP 8 and KP 12 the seabed is coarse, outside the sand wave field. There the new survey comes out 0.12 m deeper on average, with a scatter of 0.04 m: a constant, not a movement of the seabed. It is corrected before any other calculation.

In four kilometres out of eight the cover falls below the minimum over at least one stretch, down to 0.72 m between KP 13 and KP 14. The 2021 minimum, 1.10 m, lay between KP 17 and KP 18, which today is not the worst section: the seabed does not drop where the cover was already low, but where the troughs arrive.

Correcting the constant is not a detail. Without it, the same four kilometres would give 1180 m below the minimum instead of 850, 39% more: an error in the new survey attributed to the seabed, in the same direction along the whole route.

The objections that will come

The first: the cable was installed compliant, and the report proves it. True, and not the question. The report proves how the work was done; the due diligence has to establish the condition of what is being bought. A cable laid to the highest standard can have 0.72 m of cover today: the two do not contradict each other, and the acquisition concerns the second.

The second: the design already allowed for mobility, setting the burial relative to a reference level the seabed is not expected to go below. In that case the object of the check changes, not the method: the 2026 seabed is compared with that level instead of with the installation seabed, and an absolute level is still needed to do it. In the example the criterion referred to the installation seabed, and the report said so.

The third: two different surveys cannot simply be subtracted, and the difference lies within their uncertainties. The vertical constant has already been removed on the stable section, with a residual scatter of 0.04 m. That leaves the cable position, known from the as-built with an uncertainty of 0.5 m: on a sand wave flank sloping at 8°, half a metre of error in plan is worth 0.5 × tan 8° = 0.07 m in level. Together they come to 0.08 m. Even at the two least clear-cut points, at 0.87 m, the shortfall from the minimum is 0.13 m, larger than that uncertainty; at the worst point it is 0.28 m.

What the due diligence produces

Not a verdict on the cable, but a list: four sections, their length, the minimum cover in each. That is the form in which the data serves whoever has to decide whether to plan additional protection, survey more often or take it into account in the negotiation. A report that says "compliant" serves none of the three, even when it is true.

And the example is the lucky case. Had the data room contained only the cover report, without the cable levels, the multibeam survey would have shown that the seabed has moved, but not how much the cover has fallen, because one of the two terms of the subtraction is missing.

What to require in the data room

  • 01The absolute level of the top of the cable, not only the cover. Point by point, with the vertical datum stated. It is the data that allows the cover to be updated with a bathymetric survey instead of a new tracking campaign.
  • 02The as-built seabed surface, as a grid. In the same vertical reference as the cable levels. Without it, the stated cover can be neither rebuilt nor checked.
  • 03The date of every dataset, and every later survey. Intermediate monitoring surveys show where and how fast the seabed moves, and make it possible to tell a trend from an event.
  • 04The position uncertainty of the cable, with the system used to track it. On sand wave flanks an error in plan becomes an error in level, and has to be set beside the shortfall from the minimum.
  • 05The burial design criterion. Cover relative to the installation seabed, or relative to a reference level: it decides what today's seabed must be compared with.

The underlying point

An inherited dataset is read with two dates: the one on which it was acquired and the one on which it is needed. Between them, what moves has to be measured again, and what does not move has to be requested in a form that allows it.

In the example the second condition decides everything. The new survey costs the same with or without the cable levels, but only with the levels does it produce an answer. That is why the first request of a due diligence is not about the surveys to be done, but about the data the seller already has.
